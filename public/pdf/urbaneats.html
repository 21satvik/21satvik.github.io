Part 2 — Predictive Analysis Demand Forecasting
Methodology

Holt-Winters Exponential Smoothing

Holt-Winters was selected to capture the strong seasonal patterns observed across all districts — seasonality strength of 73–92%, with December demand consistently 30–40% above the annual average.

Models were configured per district as either Additive Trend / Additive Seasonal or Additive Trend / Multiplicative Seasonal depending on validation performance. Three smoothing parameters were individually optimised per district — Alpha (α) for level sensitivity, Beta (β) for trend sensitivity, and Gamma (γ) for seasonal pattern updating. Values ranged from 0.01 to 0.78 across districts.

α
Level Smoothing — Alpha

Controls sensitivity to recent changes in demand level. High values respond quickly to new patterns; low values smooth short-term volatility. Example: District 4 α=0.1107, District 6w α=0.0329.

β
Trend Smoothing — Beta

Determines sensitivity to whether demand is increasing or decreasing over time. Example: District 4 β=0.0722, District 6w β=0.0449.

γ
Seasonal Smoothing — Gamma

Controls how seasonal patterns are updated. Lower values preserve stable seasonal baselines. Example: District 4 γ=0.1531, District 6w γ=0.1751.

Board Recommendation
Open D8 & D20 immediately for rapid ROI; stagger D12, D16, D24 pending demand validation.
  • D8 first: Highest demand district (24,086 in 2026), stable growth trajectory, addresses D2's most significant remote customer burden.
  • D20 simultaneously: Cost-efficient at €200K, captures 20,435 customers and directly relieves D10's competitive overlap issue.
  • SW corridor (D12, D24): Resolves the 235,767-customer gap identified in Part 1. Stage after D8/D20 to validate southwest demand recovery.
  • €220K contingency retained: Operational buffer for fit-out overruns or demand-validation adjustments before committing remaining locations.
