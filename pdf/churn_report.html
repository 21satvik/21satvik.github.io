Executive Summary Section 01
Overview

Background and Scope

The dataset covers 7,043 telecommunications customers with a 26.5% churn rate, representing approximately €556K in annual revenue at risk. This report documents the analytical process from initial data assessment through to a costed retention recommendation, with the best-performing model achieving 84.6% AUC and the proposed strategy projecting €389K in annual revenue saved at an estimated 1,280% ROI.

The analysis runs in three stages: an exploratory phase that quantifies the problem and isolates its drivers; a modelling phase that compares three classifiers and selects the strongest; and a business impact phase that translates model outputs into a concrete, costed retention recommendation.

Key Findings
01
Contract type is the dominant churn signal
Month-to-month customers churn at 42% — fifteen times the rate of two-year contract customers (2.8%). Converting a customer from month-to-month to any fixed-term contract is the single highest-value retention action available.
02
Fiber optic customers are high-value and high-risk
Fiber optic subscribers churn at 41.9% — more than twice the rate of DSL customers. These tend to be higher-paying customers, which means each lost customer has an above-average revenue impact.
02
Electronic check is a proxy for disengagement
Electronic check users churn at 45.3% — nearly triple the rate of auto-payment customers. This pattern likely reflects customers who never fully committed to the service. Nudging customers towards automatic payment methods may reduce churn independently of contract type.
04
Tenure is inversely correlated with churn risk
Correlation analysis confirms tenure (r = 0.35) as the strongest numerical predictor of churn. The first 12 months are critical — new customers are disproportionately likely to leave. Early-stage engagement programs would have the highest marginal impact.
Predictive Modelling Section 03
Methodology

Model Development and Comparison

Three classifiers were trained and evaluated on an 80/20 stratified train-test split. Logistic Regression used standardised features; Random Forest and XGBoost operated on unscaled inputs. Cross-validation (5-fold) was used alongside AUC to assess generalisability.

XGBoost was selected as the production model. Its gradient boosting architecture handles the non-linear relationships between features (particularly the interaction between contract type, tenure, and service type) more effectively than logistic regression, while its interpretable feature importance scores make the output actionable for business teams.

Risk Segmentation

Customers were segmented into three risk tiers based on predicted churn probability. Campaign spend and messaging can be tiered accordingly — highest-value interventions reserved for the highest-risk segment.

Strategic Recommendations
Three actions that move the needle on retention.
  • Target month-to-month customers with contract upgrade incentives in their first 90 days. The churn rate gap between month-to-month (42%) and one-year contracts (11%) represents the highest-value conversion available.
  • Flag electronic check users as secondary retention targets — their 45% churn rate is a behavioural signal, not just a payment preference. Migrating them to auto-pay reduces friction and likely reduces churn independently.
  • Prioritise early-tenure customers (0–12 months) for onboarding engagement. Tenure is the second-strongest churn predictor — the first year is where customers decide whether to stay.
Conclusion Section 05
Summary

Summary and Next Steps

The XGBoost classifier achieves 84.6% AUC on the held-out test set, with cross-validation confirming stability across folds. Applied to the full customer base, the model identifies approximately 1,560 high-risk customers, enabling a targeted retention campaign with an estimated cost of €29,700 against projected savings of €389,000 annually.

The analysis identifies three primary structural drivers of churn: contract type, internet service type, and payment method. All three are actionable variables, forming the basis for the recommendations in Section 04.
